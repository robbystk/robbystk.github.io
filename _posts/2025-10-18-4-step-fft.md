## Background
The [4-step FFT], also known as Bailey's FFT algorithm,
is a method for computing the fast Fourier transform
on data stored in computer memory.

[4-step FFT]: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey's_FFT_algorithm

It involves treating the 1-dimensional signal to be transformed
as a grid of samples and transforming the rows and columns separately,
with a multiplication by specific roots of unity in between.

## Proof of Soundness

Let $$N=16$$, $$x[n]$$ be the input signal and $$X[k]$$ be its FFT. The
intermediate steps will be represented by $$Z_1$$--$$Z_4$$, indexed by $$i$$
and $$j \in \{0,1,2,3\}$$. $$W_N \equiv e^{-\mathbf{i}\tau/N}$$ (where
$$\mathbf{i} \equiv \sqrt{-1}$$ and $$\tau \equiv 2\pi$$).

First we read in the signal in row-major order:
\\[
Z_1[i,j] = x[4i+j]
\\]
Then we do FFTs column-wise:
\\[
Z_2\[i,j\] = \sum_{k=0}^3 Z_1[k,j]  W_4^{ik}
\\]
\\[
= \sum_{k=0}^3 x[j+4k]\, W_4^{ik}
\\]
Then apply "twiddle factors":
\\[
Z_3[i,j] = W_{16}^{ij}\, Z_2[i,j]
\\]
\\[
= W_{16}^{ij}\, \sum_{k=0}^3 x[j+4k]\, W_4^{ik}
\\]
And FFT row-wise:
\\[
Z_4[i,j] = \sum_{l=0}^3 Z_3[i, l]\, W_4^{jl} \\
\\]
\\[
= \sum_{l=0}^3 W_{16}^{il}\, \sum_{k=0}^3 x[4k+l]\, W_4^{ik}\, W_4^{jl} 
\\]
Then the result is read out in column-major order:
\\[
X[i+4j] = Z_4[i,j] \\
\\]
\\[
= \sum_{l=0}^3 W_{16}^{il}\, \sum_{k=0}^3 x[4k+l]\, W_4^{ik}\, W_4^{jl} 
\\]

Now, noting that $$W_4 = W_{16}^4$$, we can push the twiddle factor into
the inner sum and combine the $$W$$ terms:
\\[
X[i+4j] = \sum_{l=0}^3 W_{16}^{il}\, \sum_{k=0}^3 x[4k+l]\, W_{16}^{4ik}\, W_{16}^{4jl}
\\]
\\[
= \sum_{l=0}^3 \sum_{k=0}^3 x[4k+l]\, W_{16}^{il}\, W_{16}^{4ik}\, W_{16}^{4jl}
\\]
\\[
= \sum_{l=0}^3 \sum_{k=0}^3 x[4k+l]\, W_{16}^{il + 4ik + 4jl}
\\]
Since $$W_{16}^{16} = 1$$ and $$j$$ and $$k$$ are integers, we can multiply by
$$W_{16}^{16jk}$$ without changing the result and factor the exponent:
\\[
W_{16}^{il + 4ik + 4jl} = W_{16}^{16jk}\, W_{16}^{il + 4ik + 4jl}
\\]
\\[
= W_{16}^{4ik + il + 16jk + 4jl}
\\]
\\[
= W_{16}^{i(4k+l) + 4j(4k+l)}
\\]
\\[
= W_{16}^{(i + 4j)(4k + l)}
\\]
We can now change variables to $$m = i+4j$$ and $$n = 4k+l$$ and do the sum
over $$n$$ rather than the double sum over $$k$$ and $$l$$:
\\[
X[m] = \sum_{l=0}^3 \sum_{k=0}^3 x[4k+l]\, W_{16}^{(i+4j)(4k + l)}
\\]
\\[
= \sum_{n=0}^{15} x[n]\, W_{16}^{mn}
\\]
which is exactly the DFT of $$x[n]$$.

The same argument works for other values of $$N$$
so long as it factors into $$n_1$$ and $$n_2$$.
